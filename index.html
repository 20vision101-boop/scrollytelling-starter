The Shape of a Spike

A1c tells you the average. The curve tells you the story — and the curve is what you can actually change.

Part One — One number isn't the whole picture

A standard panel gives you a handful of static numbers: A1c, fasting glucose, fasting insulin, maybe a HOMA-IR score if your labs (Function, in my case) calculate it. Those numbers matter — but they're snapshots. A1c is a 3-month average; it can look fine even if you're spiking to 180 mg/dL every afternoon and crashing an hour later.

What the average hides is variability — the shape of the curve after a meal. Two people can share an identical A1c and have completely different glucose stories.

Part Four — Turning labs into a plan

A real plan stacks three layers: bloodwork (A1c, fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, triglyceride/HDL ratio from a panel like Function's), body composition (visceral fat and lean mass from a DEXA or MRI scan), and day-to-day tracking (supplement and symptom logs, a CGM if you use one — SuppCo territory). None of the three alone tells you enough.

The chart above is the mechanism worth remembering: it's not "avoid carbs," it's "control the shape of the curve" — pairing, order of eating, and post-meal movement all bend it without cutting anything out.
