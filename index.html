01 · THE PREMISE

Part One — One number isn't the whole picture

A standard panel gives you a handful of static numbers: A1c, fasting glucose, fasting insulin, maybe a HOMA-IR score if your labs (Function, in my case) calculate it. Those numbers matter — but they're snapshots. A1c is a 3-month average; it can look fine even if you're spiking to 180 mg/dL every afternoon and crashing an hour later.

What the average hides is variability — the shape of the curve after a meal. Two people can share an identical A1c and have completely different glucose stories.

03 · THE FOOD

Part Three — Not all carbs spike equally

Glycemic index (GI) ranks how fast a carb raises blood glucose on its own, 0-100. Glycemic load (GL) adjusts that for a realistic portion — which is why watermelon has a high GI but a low GL. Bars below are GL; each food's GI is labeled underneath. 04 · THE SUPPLEMENTS

Part Four — Supplements people pair with this

None of these replace the food choices above — they're add-ons some people layer in and track (SuppCo territory), usually worth checking with a doctor first given interaction risk with diabetes medication.

Berberine

Activates AMPK, a cellular energy sensor; studied alongside metformin for effects on insulin sensitivity and fasting glucose.

Resistant starch

Resists digestion (cooled potatoes/rice, green bananas) and ferments in the colon, blunting the glycemic response of the meal it's part of.

Apple cider vinegar

Acetic acid may slow gastric emptying and blunt the glucose response when taken before a carb-heavy meal.

Chromium picolinate

A cofactor for insulin receptor function, most studied in people who already have insulin resistance.

Magnesium

Involved in insulin receptor signaling. A large share of adults run low, and low magnesium tracks with worse insulin sensitivity.

Alpha-lipoic acid

An antioxidant studied for improving insulin sensitivity, particularly in diabetic neuropathy research.

05 · THE PLAN

Part Five — Turning labs into a plan

A real plan stacks three layers: bloodwork (A1c, fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, triglyceride/HDL ratio from a panel like Function's), body composition (visceral fat and lean mass from a DEXA or MRI scan), and day-to-day tracking (the food pairing and supplements above, plus a CGM if you use one — SuppCo territory). None of the three alone tells you enough.

The chart back in Part Two is the mechanism worth remembering: it's not "avoid carbs," it's "control the shape of the curve" — pairing, order of eating, post-meal movement, and the right supplement layer all bend it without cutting anything out.
